# Empresas en 24 Horas (E24H)

**Empresas en 24 Horas (E24H)** is the Dominican government's program (led
by **MICM** with the **Consejo Nacional de Competitividad – Burocracia
Cero**, **ONAPI**, **DGII** and the Chambers of Commerce) to compress the
process of opening a business — as a Sole Proprietorship, EIRL, or SRL —
from weeks down to a single day. I worked across both public-facing pieces
of it: the informational site (live at `empresas24h.gob.do`) and the
application form that lives inside the government portal, where the actual
request is filed with the three institutions.

## Overview

### The Challenge

A citizen deciding to formalize a business needs two very different things
at once: a clear, convincing explanation of the program (fees, required
documents, what each legal structure means) and a working form that
actually reaches ONAPI, DGII, and the Chamber of Commerce without losing
data along the way. Building for one without the other doesn't hold up — a
landing page that promises "24 hours" is only honest if the form behind it
delivers.

Key capabilities across the two fronts:

- **The informational site** — fee calculator, step-by-step guides per legal
  structure (PF/EIRL/SRL), a downloadable-document library, an institutional
  resources section, and a rate-limited contact form.
- **The application form** — a multi-step request flow per legal structure,
  submission to three institutions in parallel, per-institution status
  tracking, and document handling for issued certificates.

## My Process

### My Role

I built the informational site end-to-end from a design handoff, matching
the visual system closely and shipping a few things beyond the page
itself: build-time social-card generation, privacy-friendly analytics, and
a rate-limited contact form.

On the application form inside the government portal, I contributed ongoing
improvements throughout the beta: fixing data-submission issues with the
partner institutions, improving how applicants see the status of their
request, making a few fields easier to correct after submission, and
helping tighten the flow ahead of public beta.

### Built With

- [Astro](https://astro.build/) + [TypeScript](https://www.typescriptlang.org/), [Tailwind CSS](https://tailwindcss.com/), [Zod](https://zod.dev/)
- [Satori](https://github.com/vercel/satori) + [Sharp](https://sharp.pixelplumbing.com/) for build-time OG images
- [OpenPanel](https://openpanel.dev/) for cookieless, self-hosted analytics
- [Next.js](https://nextjs.org/) + [React](https://react.dev/) + TypeScript, [Tailwind CSS](https://tailwindcss.com/), [Sass](https://sass-lang.com/)
- [Formik](https://formik.org/) + [Yup](https://github.com/jquense/yup) for the multi-step application form and its validation
- Docker for delivery

### What I Learned

Working both fronts at once made the connection between them concrete: a
marketing site and a government form are usually treated as separate
problems, but here they're one promise to the same citizen. It sharpened my
approach to design-to-code fidelity and build-time asset pipelines, and it
was a good reminder that in a government service, even a small fix can
matter a lot to the person on the other end of it.
