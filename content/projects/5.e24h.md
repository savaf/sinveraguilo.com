# Empresas en 24 Horas (E24H)

**Empresas en 24 Horas** is the Dominican government's program (led by
**MICM** with the **Consejo Nacional de Competitividad – Burocracia Cero**,
**ONAPI**, **DGII** and the Chambers of Commerce) to compress the process of
opening a business — as a Sole Proprietorship, EIRL, or SRL — from weeks
down to a single day. I worked on both public-facing pieces of it: the
informational site, **CE24H** (`creatuempresa.gob.do`, live at
`empresas24h.gob.do`), and the transactional engine that actually files the
request with the three institutions, known internally in the codebase as the
**VUF** (_Ventanilla Única de Formalización_) — to citizens, it's simply the
same "Empresas en 24 Horas" service.

## Overview

### The Challenge

A citizen deciding to formalize a business needs two very different things
at once: a clear, convincing explanation of the program (fees, required
documents, what each legal structure means) and a working form that
actually reaches ONAPI, DGII, and the Chamber of Commerce without losing
data along the way. Building for one without the other doesn't hold up — a
landing page that promises "24 hours" is only honest if the form behind it
delivers.

Key capabilities across the two fronts:

- **CE24H (informational site)** — fee calculator, step-by-step guides per
  legal structure (PF/EIRL/SRL), a downloadable-document library, an
  institutional resources section, and a rate-limited contact form.
- **VUF (transactional engine)** — a multi-step request flow per legal
  structure, submission to three institutions in parallel, per-institution
  status tracking (including objection and return states), and document
  handling for ONAPI-issued certificates.

## My Process

### My Role

On **CE24H**, I built the Astro site end-to-end from an Illustrator design
handoff:

- A **pixel-accurate scaling system** — the design is laid out on a 1920px
  artboard, so I made the root `rem` scale with the viewport
  (`clamp(12px, 0.8333vw, 16px)`), meaning every measurement from the design
  translates 1:1 as `design_px / 16` rem at desktop sizes, with a separate
  fixed scale below 1024px.
- **Build-time OG image generation** — social cards for every business
  structure and library article are composed with **Satori** and
  rasterized with **Sharp**, decompressing the site's `woff2` fonts in
  memory for that one step so nothing extra ships to the runtime.
- **Cookieless analytics** — migrated the self-hosted **OpenPanel**
  integration to its official Astro SDK and instrumented the main
  conversion CTA.
- A **rate-limited contact form** running behind a reverse proxy, respecting
  `X-Forwarded-*` so the per-IP limit stays meaningful.

On the **VUF**, my work was a steady stream of fixes and small features
that mattered a lot to whoever was mid-application:

- Corrected the `cargo` code sent to ONAPI for SRL partners with no role
  assigned (`'0'` → the real SOCIO code, `'10'`), so requests stopped
  arriving misclassified.
- Fixed a saved-SRL-form bug that dropped the applicant's `cédula` on
  reopening.
- Added a fallback for the ONAPI objection document link, so there's always
  a document to open, objection or not.
- Added status badges distinguishing **Objetado** from **Devuelto** across
  all three institutions, instead of both reading the same.
- Added a pre-submission checklist and unlocked the applicant's
  name/surname for correction before sending.
- Tightened the form for public beta: disabled passport as a document type
  (not yet supported end-to-end), removed invalid SRL partner relationship
  types, and switched ONAPI advisory contact from phone to WhatsApp.

### What I Learned

Working both fronts at once made the connection between them concrete: a
marketing site and a government form are usually treated as separate
problems, but here they're one promise to the same citizen. The Astro side
sharpened my approach to design-to-code fidelity and build-time asset
pipelines; the VUF side was a reminder that in a government service, a
"small" bug — a missing cargo code, a lost cédula, an ambiguous status — is
never small to the person on the other end of it.
